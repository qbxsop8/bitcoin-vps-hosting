## Why Pay for a VPS with Bitcoin?

Let's be honest about what Bitcoin payments do and don't give you.

When you pay for a VPS with a credit card, the paper trail is straightforward: card issuer knows you bought a server, the hosting company has your billing info, and everything is linked to your real identity. For most people running personal projects or small businesses, this isn't a concern at all.

But there are plenty of legitimate situations where reducing that trail matters:

- **Privacy by default** — Some users just prefer not to have hosting accounts linked to financial identity, the same way people use cash for purchases.
- **Cross-border convenience** — In many regions, international card payments for hosting are blocked, declined, or subject to extra verification. Crypto sidesteps that entirely.
- **Crypto-native projects** — If you're running a Monero node, a self-hosted wallet, or any crypto-adjacent service, paying in crypto is just consistent.
- **Financial sovereignty** — Hosting paid with crypto can't be taken down by payment processor pressure the way card-linked accounts potentially can.

A few important caveats though: Bitcoin itself is not perfectly anonymous. On-chain BTC transactions are publicly visible. If you buy Bitcoin from an exchange that has your KYC info and then send it directly to a hosting provider, the link exists. For stronger anonymity, people use Monero, or mix BTC, or buy through P2P without KYC. That's a separate rabbit hole — but the point is, VPS Bitcoin payment is meaningfully more private than card payment for most users, even if it's not a complete shield.

## What to Look for in a VPS That Accepts Bitcoin

Not all "crypto-friendly" VPS providers are created equal. Here's what actually matters:

**1. Which cryptocurrencies are accepted?**

Bitcoin is the baseline. Some providers also accept Litecoin, Ethereum, Monero, USDT, and others. More options give you flexibility on transaction fees and privacy level.

**2. Is payment processing in-house or via a third party?**

Some providers route payments through CoinPayments or similar gateways. This means the gateway has a record of your transaction even if the hosting company doesn't ask for ID. In-house crypto processing means fewer intermediaries.

**3. What personal info is required at signup?**

Most providers still require an email address. Some require a real name. Very few require ID verification (KYC). The less required, the better for privacy. 

**4. Is the server performance and price actually competitive?**

There's a segment of "anonymous VPS" providers that charge a premium for the privacy angle but deliver mediocre hardware. You shouldn't have to pay a privacy tax — good performance at honest prices should be baseline.

**5. Are billing terms transparent?**

Hidden fees, bandwidth overages, and CPU burst charges are how some budget hosts make their margins. Transparent, flat-rate pricing is what you want.
